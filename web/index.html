How Pay Dirt works

A tiny AI learns a famous puzzle by mining for gold.

There are three tunnels and only one has gold. The crew picks one. Then someone may light dynamite in one of the other two tunnels. That tunnel blows up and is out of play. Now the miner decides: stay with the tunnel he picked, or switch to the one that’s left.

This is the Monty Hall puzzle from the old TV game show. The famous answer is “always switch,” but that only works if whoever set off the blast knew where the gold was. Here it depends on who did it:

    Nobody tells the miners who is lighting the dynamite in each part of the mine. They have to figure it out from what happens.

    The miners

    • Rookie is the AI. A small neural network that starts out knowing nothing and learns from every pick.
    • Old Timer knows every character’s habits and does the math perfectly. The best anyone can do.
    • Bookworm always switches after a blast, because that’s the answer in the book.

    Is the Rookie learning?

    Gold each miner has missed so far, compared with someone who always knew who set off the blast. A line that goes flat means that miner stopped making mistakes.

    Test questions

    Every ten picks the Rookie answers three questions it never practices on. If it gets them right, it learned the rule instead of getting lucky.
